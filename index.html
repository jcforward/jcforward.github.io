Now

I am an incoming PhD student at Harvard University in the Systems, Synthetic, and Quantitative Biology Program (SSQB), and a Herchel Smith Graduate Fellow. I am broadly interested in how biological form and function arise from the interactions between seemingly random processes.

Here is a Curriculum Vitae.

Previously

In past lives, I was a postgraduate researcher with Drs. Fred Chang and John Vaughen at UCSF and an undergraduate fellow with Drs. Kayla King and Brett Finlay at UBC. At UCSF, I studied the physical properties of the cytoplasm in single cells and multicellular tissues. This work led me into some side adventures at MIT where I weighed single cells, Stanford with Lucy O'Brien's lab where I examined cytoplasmic organization in fly gut cells, and the MBL at Woods Hole where I just plsyed around with fancy microscopes. At UBC, I researched bacterial morphogenesis and developed methods to engineer undomesticated bacteria.
